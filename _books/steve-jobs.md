This is a book about a remarkable person living a remarkable life. I hope that in 
reality, his brilliance came more from vision than from manipulation — but the book 
doesn't shy away from showing both sides.

What struck me most about the young Steve Jobs was how his environment shaped his 
thinking. He grew up in a world that was rapidly changing, full of unexplored 
possibilities and open doors. That context seemed to imprint a certain fearlessness 
into him. That said, I'm not suggesting anyone can replicate what he did — because 
no one else has.

What I admire is his relentless curiosity and his refusal to accept things as they 
are. He had a rare ability to chase whatever he was drawn to until he found an answer 
that satisfied him.

What I struggle with is the coldness. I believe that as a person becomes more 
analytical and results-driven, the pressure to treat people as instruments rather than 
as human beings grows stronger. Manipulation might be a tool in the playbook of 
power — but adopting it as a personal identity is a different matter entirely. It can 
quietly erode the very vision you are trying to build.

If I were ever to lead something, I would want to carry some of what he had — the 
clarity, the standards, the refusal to settle — but I would want to add more humanity 
to it. Then again, I wasn't in that arena, so perhaps this is just a note to my 
future self.
